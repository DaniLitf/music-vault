# Richard D. James

**Electronic musician, producer, composer. Born August 18, 1971.**

Richard David James is a British electronic musician, producer, and composer known for his pioneering work across multiple aliases, most notably [[Artists/A/Aphex Twin]]. His music spans ambient, techno, IDM, drill 'n' bass, acid, and experimental styles. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in electronic music.

## Biography

Born in Limerick, Ireland to Welsh and Irish parents, James grew up in Cornwall, England where he began making music with tape recorders and homemade synthesizers. He emerged in the early 1990s with Aphex Twin, releasing the landmark *Selected Ambient Works 85–92* in 1992. He founded the label Rephlex Records with Grant Wilson-Claridge in 1991, which became a home for his own work and that of other innovative electronic artists.

Throughout his career James has released music under numerous aliases, each representing a different stylistic direction — from the drill 'n' bass experiments of AFX to the ambient techno of Polygon Window and the playful, sample-based work of Caustic Window. In 2014 his unreleased Caustic Window LP was sold at auction for $46,300. His 2001 Aphex Twin album *Drukqs* was inspired by his fear of an imminent death from a tumour that was later found to be benign. He won the Mercury Prize for *Richard D. James Album* in 1996 (awarded in 1997, when the prize was chronological) and a Grammy for *Syro* in 2014.
