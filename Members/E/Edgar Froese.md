**Keyboardist and composer. Born 6 June 1944; died 20 January 2015.**

Edgar Froese was a German keyboardist, composer, and electronic music pioneer best known as the founder and only continuous member of Tangerine Dream, the influential progressive and electronic rock band. Froese's visionary approach to synthesizer music and his role in developing the ambient and electronic genres made him one of the most significant figures in modern music. His compositional work defined the sound of electronic music for decades and influenced countless artists across multiple genres.

- [[Artists/T/Tangerine Dream]] – keyboardist, founder, continuous member (1967–2015)
- [[Artists/T/The Ones]] – keyboardist, psychedelic rock band (1965–1967)

## Biography

Froese founded Tangerine Dream in 1967 after being involved in the psychedelic rock band The Ones. From the band's inception, he was the driving creative force, establishing an experimental approach to rock music utilizing early synthesizers and sequencers. His pioneering work with Moog and Buchla synthesizers helped define the progressive rock and electronic music movements. Albums such as "Phaedra" (1974), "Rubycon" (1975), and "Stratosfear" (1976) became classics of electronic music. Beyond Tangerine Dream, Froese released numerous solo albums throughout the 1970s, 1980s, 1990s, 2000s, and 2010s, exploring diverse compositional approaches and electronic textures. He remained the sole founding member of Tangerine Dream until his death in 2015, maintaining creative control and the band's artistic direction throughout its history.
