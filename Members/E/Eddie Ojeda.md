**Guitarist and songwriter. Born 5 August 1955.**

Eddie Ojeda is an American guitarist best known as a founding and core member of Twisted Sister, one of the most iconic heavy metal bands of the 1980s. Nicknamed "Fingers" for his distinctive guitarwork, Ojeda was a key architect of Twisted Sister's signature dual-guitar attack and heavy, theatrical sound. His energetic stage presence and technical skill became defining elements of the band's legendary live performances.

## Biography

Ojeda co-founded Twisted Sister in the mid-1970s and helped develop their distinctive brand of heavy metal, characterized by dual-guitar harmonies, theatrical imagery, and powerful vocals. During the 1980s, Twisted Sister achieved international success with albums like "Under the Blade" (1982), "Stay Hungry" (1984), and "Come Out and Play" (1985), becoming stadium headliners. Ojeda's guitarsmanship and songwriting were crucial to the band's commercial and critical appeal. After Twisted Sister's initial dissolution in 1988, he pursued various projects including the band Scarecrow and released a solo album, "Axes to Axes" (2005). He reunited with Twisted Sister for tours and recordings beginning in 2003, continuing to perform with the band until its final tour in 2016.
