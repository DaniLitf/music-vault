# Alice Cooper

**Lead vocalist, frontman, and sole constant member of the Alice Cooper band and later solo artist. Born 1948-02-04.**

Alice Cooper (born Vincent Damon Furnier) is an American singer and songwriter known as the "Godfather of Shock Rock." He fronted the Alice Cooper band from 1968 to 1975, then launched a successful solo career after legally changing his name to Alice Cooper. His theatrical stage shows, raspy vocals, and hits like "School's Out," "I'm Eighteen," "No More Mr. Nice Guy," and "Poison" have made him one of rock's most enduring icons.

The original Alice Cooper band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2011. Cooper has sold over 50 million records worldwide and continues to tour and record. He is also a member of the supergroup Hollywood Vampires alongside Joe Perry and Johnny Depp.

## Biography

Vincent Damon Furnier was born in Detroit, Michigan, and moved to Phoenix, Arizona as a child. He formed his first band, the Earwigs, in 1964 for a high school talent show. The band evolved through several name changes (the Spiders, Nazz) before settling on Alice Cooper in 1968. The original band broke up in 1975, after which Furnier legally changed his name to Alice Cooper and continued as a solo artist.

His solo debut, *Welcome to My Nightmare* (1975), was a critical and commercial success. After struggling with alcoholism in the late 1970s and early 1980s, Cooper made a major comeback with *Trash* (1989) and its hit single "Poison." He has since maintained a prolific recording and touring schedule, releasing 30 studio albums over his career. In 2015, he co-founded the supergroup Hollywood Vampires.
