**Record producer, singer, songwriter, and pioneering figure in reggae and dub music. Born 20 March 1936, died 29 August 2021.**

Lee "Scratch" Perry was a Jamaican record producer, singer, and songwriter, one of the most innovative and influential figures in reggae and dub music. He began his career in the late 1950s working for Clement Coxsone Dodd at Studio One, where he recorded nearly thirty songs before leaving amid disputes. After a brief stint with Joe Gibbs, he formed his own Upsetter label in 1968 and assembled the Upsetters session band, producing a prolific body of work throughout the late 1960s and early 1970s. In 1973, he built the Black Ark studio in his backyard, where he created his most celebrated productions — a string of classic albums for the Congos, Junior Murvin, Max Romeo, the Heptones, and Bob Marley and the Wailers. After the Black Ark burned down around 1980, Perry spent decades touring and recording with a new generation of collaborators including Adrian Sherwood, Mad Professor, the Beastie Boys, the Orb, and many others.

Perry was awarded the Order of Distinction (Commander class) by the Jamaican government in 2012 and won a Grammy for Best Reggae Album in 2003 for *Jamaican E.T.* In 2004, Rolling Stone ranked him number 100 on their list of the 100 Greatest Artists of All Time. He continued recording and performing into his 80s, residing in Switzerland until his death in 2021.
