**Record producer, songwriter, and singer. Jamaican.**

Lee "Scratch" Perry was a Jamaican record producer, songwriter, and singer, one of the most innovative and influential figures in reggae and dub music. Known for his revolutionary studio techniques, surrealist approach to recording, and eccentric persona, he pioneered the development of dub music in the 1970s through his early adoption of remixing and studio effects. He worked at Coxsone Dodd's Studio One in the 1960s before founding his Upsetter label and building the legendary Black Ark studio in 1973, where he produced a remarkable string of classic reggae albums for artists including Bob Marley and the Wailers, the Congos, Junior Murvin, Max Romeo, and the Heptones.
